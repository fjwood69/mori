# Commercial Licensing

Mori is released under the [GNU Affero General Public License v3.0](LICENSE)
(AGPL-3.0).

Under AGPL-3.0, if you run Mori as a network service — including internal
deployment where developers access it over a network — and you modify the
source code, you must release those modifications under AGPL-3.0.

A commercial licence removes this requirement.

---

## When you need a commercial licence

A commercial licence is required if you:

- Modify Mori and need to keep those modifications private
- Embed Mori in a proprietary product or platform
- Offer a hosted or managed Mori service to customers or third parties
- Are subject to organisational policies that prohibit AGPL dependencies
- Require contractual warranties, indemnification, or SLAs

---

## What a commercial licence provides

- Permission to modify Mori without AGPL's source disclosure requirements
- Permission to offer Mori as a hosted or embedded service
- A private, negotiated agreement suited to your organisation's legal requirements
- Optional: implementation support, pattern library development, enterprise onboarding

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my team use Mori without a commercial licence?**

Yes, under AGPL — but if you modify the source code and run it as a network
service, those modifications must be released under AGPL-3.0.

**We're an enterprise with a policy against AGPL. What are our options?**

Contact us. Commercial licences are available and can be structured to meet
enterprise procurement requirements.

**Can we fork Mori and build a product on top of it?**

Under AGPL, yes — but if you offer that product as a network service, your
modifications must also be released under AGPL-3.0. If you need to keep
your modifications private, a commercial licence is required.

**What counts as a network service?**

Any deployment where users — including your own developers — access Mori
over a network. This includes internal corporate deployments.
